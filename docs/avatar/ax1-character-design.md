# AX-1 character design

AX-1 is an upper-body, premium industrial humanoid designed for sign-language presentation. Its visual language uses satin white outer armour, high-gloss black visor and exposed black mechanical joints. It is deliberately human-proportioned and avoids toy, cartoon and militaristic styling.

## Communication-first silhouette

The upper torso remains narrow enough that both palms and all fingertips read clearly against the background. In the neutral pose, the hands rest forward and outside the torso silhouette. Shoulder hubs are inset, while upper arms and forearms are slim, leaving room for hand crossing and the signing space in front of the chest.

## Head and visor

The head has a white shell surrounding a continuous smoked-black visor. The shell, visor and neck are separate meshes. The visor is a presentation surface, not a claim of complete ISL non-manual grammar. Future approved expression work may use separate eye, brow and mouth-region emissive masks, material parameters, morph targets or facial bones, subject to linguistic review.

## Hands

Each hand comprises a palm, wrist collar, thumb, and four fingers. Every digit is segmented into individually named mesh parts; the thumb begins at an opposing angle. Armour does not span finger joints. This preserves the later option to use a conventional skeletal hand rig while keeping palm orientation and fingertip contact legible.

## Approval gate

This is a base-model proposal, not a production model or a linguistic approval. The design must be visually reviewed at target camera distances before skeletal rigging begins.
